Lumber, nominal and actual

Stock lengths and precut studs

Doors

Door sizes are named width then height in feet and inches: a 3068 is 3′-0″ × 6′-8″. A slab is the door leaf alone. A prehung door comes hung in its frame, and the rough opening is framed for the frame.

  • Accessible doors need 32″ of clear width with the door open 90° (ADA 2010 404.2.3). Stops and the door's own thickness take 2 to 3″ off the leaf width, so a 2′-10″ or 3′-0″ door is used.
  • Interior doors taller than 80″ or wider than 36″ are usually made 1¾″ thick.
  • The rough opening is typical framing practice. Each manufacturer publishes its own; check it before framing.

Sources: 2010 ADA Standards, 404.2.3. Door manufacturers' size charts and installation guides.

Windows

Window sizes use the same naming as doors: a 3050 is 3′-0″ wide by 5′-0″ high. Stock widths step in 6″ or 4″ and heights in 6″ or 12″, and each maker's call numbers differ from the actual unit size by a fraction of an inch.

  • Every sleeping room and habitable basement needs one. The minimum height and width together give only 3.3 sf, so one of them must be larger, which is why bedroom casements and sliders cluster around 2′-0″ to 3′-0″ wide and double-hungs around 3′-0″ × 5′-0″ and up.

  • Rough openings are typically the unit size plus ½″ in each direction. Check the maker's published rough opening.

Sources: ICC, IRC emergency escape and rescue openings; City of Saint Paul egress window handout. Window manufacturers' size tables.
