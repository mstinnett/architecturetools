What should I use to mark up and review drawings?

Updated October 2026

You don’t need a subscription to mark up drawings, measure them or check a set. Clouds, callouts and text are standard PDF markup that every app here opens. You subscribe to mark up together live with your team and consultants, and for that you use what your consultants use.

Dots are each main app’s features. Web and iPad versions often do less. Measuring to scale means calibrating a sheet to its drawing scale and taking lengths and areas off it. Prices and plans change. Check the vendor before you buy.

Already paying for Adobe

Use Acrobat Pro before buying anything else. It marks up, measures to scale, compares files and runs shared review. It has no tool chest or sortable markups list, so a fifty-sheet review takes more clicks than in Bluebeam.

Before you leave Bluebeam

Your clouds and callouts open anywhere. What stays behind is the Bluebeam layer around them. Test these on one of your own sets first:

  • Calibrate a sheet and measure a dimension you know.
  • Mark up a set in the new app and open it in Bluebeam, then the reverse. Check that clouds, callouts and colors survive.
  • Rebuild the markups you use most. Bluebeam tool chests and custom markups-list columns do not import.
  • Review one set with a consultant. If they stay on Bluebeam, decide who exports and who imports.

Review inside a project platform

If the project lives in Autodesk Docs or Procore, mark up there. The comments stay attached to the sheet version the contractor builds from.
