Roof

Read heights off a plan. Give the pitch and how far a point is from the eave line in plan, and the height of the roof there comes back, built to the fraction you set, with what the rounding costs.

Roof area

The factor is the same for every plane at one pitch, so a hip roof and a gable over the same plan have the same roof area. No waste is included.

Hips and valleys

Heights never need the hip: the nearest eave line gives them. The hip matters for its own rafter and, with unequal pitches, for where it falls in plan.

Low slope, to a drain

The curb or parapet has to stand above the high point. Add the flashing height your roofing system asks for to the taper.

A cricket is the same taper, turned across the high side of a curb or chimney so water runs around it. The IRC asks for one behind a chimney wider than 30".

Every length on this page is held as a whole number of engine units. One unit is 1/960 of a millimeter, so a millimeter is 960 units and an inch is 24,384. Whole millimeters and sixteenths, thirty-seconds and sixty-fourths of an inch are all whole numbers of units, so metric and imperial inputs are both exact, and no step rounds in decimals along the way. When a result falls between units, such as the sloped length of most roofs, it is snapped to the nearest unit and the note says by how much. The lines below are the arithmetic for the reading above.
