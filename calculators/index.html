Calculators

Dimensional tools on one exact engine — every length is an integer count of 1/960 mm, so the math never drifts. What each tool adds is the part calculators usually hide: the residual. The cut, the slack, the drift, the rounding — drawn to scale, signed, yours to judge.

These tools surface information; they don't decide. None of them will tell you a dimension is right or wrong — they show exactly what each choice costs, against the grid, the limit, or the reference you pick, and leave the call where it belongs.
On the engine
Precise Unit Converter Any dimension, any format, snapped to a fabrication grid — Min / Nearest / Max with their signed residuals on a dual-scale number line.
Run Solver Fit a repeating module into a run — tiles with joints, on-center spacing, balusters, equal sections. The leftover is drawn, flush and balanced layouts side by side.
Scale Resolution The range a measurement actually knows — read a drawing and the scale multiplies sheet error into the field; measure to draw and tape error accumulates against the sheet resolution.
Slope Any two of rise, run, and slope — the missing sides and every form back as values, the cost of rounding to whole numbers shown in rise, common slopes drawn for comparison.
Material Coverage The takeoff as you'd say it — chain rooms, subtract openings — divided by a sheet, tile, or coverage rate into a count with both ends of the rounding shown. Waste unbundled: breakage and attic stock are named choices, cut waste belongs to the layout.
Uncut Tile Run What dimension does the wall want so the tile runs uncut? A set-it-here range for each whole-tile count — the joints flex — with your rough wall located, the slack shown, and the run ends included: bullnose, metal edge, lapped corner.
Stairs Every workable riser count from a total rise, each located against the IRC or IBC figure with its citation, the section drawn to scale with the layout drift hatched.
Life safety — on the engine, citations attached
Occupant Load A typed area takeoff ÷ an IBC Table 1004.5 factor — exact quotient, the round-up the code's “not less than” implies, and the gross/net basis said out loud. Carries its count straight into egress width.
Egress Width Load × factor both ways — required width from a count, capacity from a width — with the sprinkler condition stated and the component minimums located, including when they govern.
Exits How many ways out a load needs (the 500 / 1,000 thresholds) and how far apart they must sit — the half-diagonal rule computed from your plan, both fractions always shown.
Plumbing Fixtures The IPC's banded ratios through a §403.1.1 sex split — exact quotients beside every round-up. part of the table is draft — each value carries its own tag
Accessibility
Ramp A rise becomes runs and landings at 1:12, 1:16, 1:20, or your slope — the 30″ rise-per-run cap segments the ramp, drawn in plan with the layout drift shown, every figure cited to §405.
Kickoff — programming
Homeowner Questionnaire A curated worksheet for firms to print and hand a residential client before the first working session — constraints before wishlist, activities not rooms, need/want/dream with the collisions kept. Filled in by hand; the print strips the site chrome.
Reference
Codes & Standards The reference sheet: which codes apply when — IBC or IRC, the trade codes, energy, accessibility, loads — then every standard behind these tools with editions, access, and authoritative links.
Sheet Sizes ARCH and ANSI sheets with what fits on each at every standard scale — type a footprint and the largest scale that carries it lights up, drawn on the sheet.
