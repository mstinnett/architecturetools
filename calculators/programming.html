Homeowner Questionnaire

Before our first working session, please fill this in — in pen, in your own words. There are no wrong answers, and nothing here commits you to anything. If two of your answers pull against each other, write down both: those collisions are the most useful thing you can hand your architect.

1 · The project, in your words
What do you want to do, in one or two sentences? Your words, not drawing words — "make the back of the house work for a family that lives in the kitchen."
Why now? A baby, a sale, working from home, a repair that grew. Whatever set this in motion shapes the priorities more than any wishlist.
Who makes the decisions — and who else has a real say? A spouse or partner, a parent helping fund it, an HOA board. Everyone whose yes matters, even informally.
2 · The practical frame

These set the box the design lives inside. Numbers before rooms isn't backwards — knowing the box first is what lets the design spend your money where you care most.

The budget you're actually comfortable with — a range is fine. Construction needs a contingency on top, typically 10–20% held in reserve. A number with that in mind is worth more than a hopeful one.
Any hard dates? A lease ending, a school year, a birth. "Soon" is fine — say which kind of soon.
What do you already have or know? Check what applies:
We own the property
We have a survey — the drawing of your legal lot lines
We have drawings of the existing house
There's an HOA or deed restrictions
We're on septic, not city sewer — matters early if bathrooms or bedrooms are involved
Anything fixed about the site or house we should know? A tree you'd never remove, the view you bought the house for, known problems — water, settling, old wiring.
3 · How you live

Describe activities, not rooms. "We host twelve for dinner a few times a year" says more than "big dining room" — it might not even be a dining room that solves it.

What actually happens in this place — and who does it? Cooking together or one cook? Homework at the counter? Two video calls at once? Instruments, gear, a dog that needs washing?
How many people, day to day — and at the peak? "Four daily, ten at Thanksgiving, kids' friends most Fridays."
What needs to be near what? What needs to be far apart? Bedroom away from the street; laundry near bedrooms; office where the doorbell can't end a meeting.
Walk through a normal day — morning and evening. Where do shoes, bags, mail, and the leash actually land? Where does the day bottleneck? This finds the rooms no one thinks to ask for.
4 · Need · want · dream

List everything, then mark one column for each. Need — the project fails without it. Want — real, but you'd trade it. Dream — if the budget smiles. Be stingy with Need: when everything is a need, nothing is. And don't trim the dreams — they tell us what the project is really about.

5 · Comfort, character, and the long run
Comfort and energy — what do you care about? Drafty-room grudges, a too-hot bedroom, utility bills, solar someday, quiet. Say it even without the technical name.
Should this house work for someone with stairs trouble — now or someday? A bedroom and full bath that could live on the main floor, wider doorways, a step-free way in. Planning it now is cheap; retrofitting it isn't.
How much upkeep are you honestly willing to do? "We'll oil the deck every year" and "we never will" are both fine answers — they lead to different materials.
Three images you love, one you hate — and a few words on why. Links, magazine pages, photos of places you've been. Bring them to the session; the one you hate is as useful as the three you love.
6 · Before our session

Worth gathering now — each one saves a week later. Check what you can bring; note on the line what you can't find.

Deed, and any HOA covenants or easement documents
The survey, if one exists
Any existing drawings — old permit sets, even the realtor floor plan
Budget confirmed with whoever shares the decision
If on septic: the system's location and last inspection, if known
And the questions you want to ask us. Write them down now — first meetings eat memory.
