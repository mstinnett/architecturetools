Roof Taper

How high does the taper build on a low-slope roof? Start with the flat roof in plan, place the drains, and read the height at the high points for the slope you need.

Tap the plan to add a drain, up to six. Drag a drain to move it, or off the roof to remove it, or type a dimension from its nearest corner. Each band is 1" of taper.

Six drains is the most for one roof area here. Split a larger roof into drainage areas and lay out each one.

The curb or parapet stands above the high point, plus the flashing height your roofing system calls for. A cricket is the same taper turned across the high side of a curb or chimney, so water runs around it.

A pitched roof? Roof reads heights off a section. A pipe or a gutter run? Drain takes it from its minimum slope.

Every length on this page is held as a whole number of engine units. One unit is 1/960 of a millimeter, so a millimeter is 960 units and an inch is 24,384. The taper is four-way: each drain sits at the low point of four planes that meet along diagonal crickets, the usual tapered-insulation layout. The taper at any point is the slope times the larger of its two plan distances, across and along, to the nearest drain, and where two drains meet a ridge forms halfway between them. The high point is the largest of those heights over the whole plan; it is rounded up to the fraction you build to, because built lower the roof would fall short of its slope.
