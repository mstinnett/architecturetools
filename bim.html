Architecture software, from Sketchpad to now

Timeline

Where today’s most used tools came from, the ones that rose and faded along the way, and the newcomers. Research and industrial CAD from the late 1950s, desktop drafting in the 1980s, model-based BIM from the late 1980s and 2000, 3D and animation software in the studios of the 1990s, parametric design and renderers in the 2000s and 2010s, and browser-based tools since 2020. A year is a first release unless the entry says otherwise.

First release Rename, acquisition or standard No longer sold
  1. 1950s–60s
    1. 1957[1] PRONTO Patrick Hanratty at General Electric, an early commercial language for programming machine tools
    2. 1963[2] Sketchpad Ivan Sutherland's MIT thesis: drawing on a screen with a light pen, with reusable masters and constraints
    3. 1964[3] DAC-1 General Motors and IBM's Design Augmented by Computer, in use at GM from 1963
    4. 1969[4] Computervision Founded in Bedford, Massachusetts. Its first product, CADDS, drafted in 2D
    5. 1969[5] M&S Computing Founded by former IBM engineers. Renamed Intergraph in 1980
  2. 1970s
    1. 1974[6] Building Description System Charles Eastman's Carnegie-Mellon report: a building as a database of 3D elements, the idea BIM grew from
    2. 1977[7] RUCAPS Building models from the London architects GMW, an early ancestor of BIM. Its successors Sonata and Reflex were gone by the late 1990s
    3. 1977[8] GLIDE Eastman and Henrion's language for building design databases, shown at SIGGRAPH
    4. 1977[9] CATI Dassault's in-house 3D design system, renamed CATIA in 1981. Gehry's office later built on it
  3. 1980s
    1. 1982[10] AutoCAD First released in December, for CP/M and the IBM PC
    2. 1984[11] Radar CH Graphisoft, for the Apple Lisa. Renamed ArchiCAD in 1987
    3. 1984[12] Allplan Nemetschek, 3D building models for architects and engineers
    4. 1985[13] MiniCAD Graphsoft, on the Mac. Renamed VectorWorks later
    5. 1986[14] MicroStation Bentley Systems, version 1, after PseudoStation in 1985
    6. 1987[15] ArchiCAD Radar CH released as ArchiCAD, the "Virtual Building"
    7. 1988[16] Softimage 3D animation software, later one of the first two packages in Columbia's paperless studios
  4. 1990s
    1. 1991[17] form·Z auto·des·sys, Columbus, Ohio. Solid and surface modeling on the Mac, used in the first paperless studios. Still sold
    2. 1992[18] Chief Architect Home design on Windows 3.1
    3. 1992[19] “Building information model” An early published use of the term, by van Nederveen and Tolman
    4. 1994[20] Paperless studios Columbia's architecture school opens digital studios on Alias and Softimage. Greg Lynn teaches one of the first
    5. 1997[21] IFC First published IFC release, the open building-model exchange standard
    6. 1997[22] Architectural Desktop Autodesk's architectural objects on AutoCAD. Renamed AutoCAD Architecture in 2007, now a toolset inside AutoCAD
    7. 1997[23] 3D Studio VIZ A version of 3D Studio MAX for architects. Folded into 3ds Max Design in 2008
    8. 1997[24] Piranesi Informatix, Cambridge. Paints hand-drawn-style renderings over 3D models. Sales ended in October 2025
    9. 1998[25] Rhino McNeel, version 1.0 in October
    10. 1998[26] VectorWorks MiniCAD renamed at version 8
    11. 1998[27] Maya Alias|Wavefront's animation software, used for curved forms by Greg Lynn and at Zaha Hadid Architects
  5. 2000s
    1. 2000[28] Revit Revit Technology Corporation, version 1 in April
    2. 2000[29] SketchUp @Last Software, in August
    3. 2002[30] V-Ray Chaos, first version, for 3ds Max
    4. 2002[31] Revit to Autodesk Bought for $133 million
    5. 2002[32] “BIM” white paper Autodesk publishes Building Information Modeling, and the term spreads
    6. 2003[33] GenerativeComponents Bentley's parametric modeling, shaped by SmartGeometry members from Foster + Partners, KPF and Arup.
    7. 2004[34] Digital Project Gehry Technologies, building design on CATIA
    8. 2005[35] Twinmotion KA-RA, founded 2004. A second version was out by about 2008
    9. 2005[36] buildingSMART The IFC alliance takes its current name
    10. 2006[37] SketchUp to Google Bought from @Last Software
    11. 2007[38] Grasshopper Released in September as Explicit History, for Rhino
    12. 2008[39] Ecotect to Autodesk Andrew Marsh's building performance analysis. Retired in 2015, its features moved into Revit
  6. 2010s
    1. 2010[40] Lumion Act-3D, in December
    2. 2010[41] Project Vasari Free Autodesk Labs preview for massing and energy analysis, built on Revit. Retired in 2015
    3. 2011[42] Dynamo Visual programming for Revit, begun by Ian Keough. Shown in 2009, staffed by Autodesk in 2011
    4. 2012[43] SketchUp to Trimble Bought from Google
    5. 2012[44] FormIt Autodesk's massing app for the iPad, exporting to Revit. In maintenance since 2024, with Forma as its successor
    6. 2014[45] Softimage ends Autodesk announces the 2015 version will be the last
    7. 2014[46] BricsCAD BIM BIM tools in BricsCAD V15
    8. 2015[47] Corona Corona 1.0 for 3ds Max from Render Legion; Chaos bought it in 2017
    9. 2015[48] Enscape Real-time rendering inside Revit, first as a beta
    10. 2015[49] Speckle Begins as open-source research at UCL's Bartlett
    11. 2016[50] Spacemaker Founded in Oslo, AI site planning
    12. 2018[51] Hypar Founded by Ian Keough and Anthony Hauck
    13. 2018[52] Higharc Founded, design software for homebuilders
    14. 2019[53] Twinmotion to Epic Games Bought from Abvent and made free for most firms
  7. 2020s
    1. 2020[54] D5 Render Commercial release in 2020; the developer dates its anniversary to June
    2. 2020[55] Spacemaker to Autodesk Bought for $240 million
    3. 2021[56] Rayon Founded in Paris, shared 2D plans in the browser
    4. 2022[57] Snaptrude Version 1.0 in December, founded 2017
    5. 2022[58] Enscape joins Chaos The makers of Enscape and V-Ray merge
    6. 2023[59] Qonic Open beta, from the founders of Bricsys
    7. 2023[60] Forma Spacemaker released as Autodesk Forma
    8. 2025[61] Motif First product, browser model review, in March
    9. 2025[62] Arcol Public launch in June, founded 2021
    10. 2026[63] Forma Building Design Schematic design in Forma, included with Revit
    11. 2026[64] Forma to replace Revit Autodesk says "over time", with no date

Space to watch

Announcements from the last two years, newest first, from AEC Magazine, the vendors and the trade press, and what people are building and talking about outside them, marked Community.

  1. 19 Oct 2026

    Community BIMopen, a free BIM modeler written in Rust by one developer with Claude Code, publishes its first alpha builds for the browser, Windows and Linux. It opens Revit exchange, and its source is due under an open license. [153]

  2. 13 Oct 2026

    Community CadCraft publishes eight open-source CAD and BIM apps as early test builds, covering drafting, BIM, civil, takeoff and markup, each meant to feel familiar to Autodesk users. [154]

  3. 11 Oct 2026

    Community The maker of ArtCraft, free Adobe clones built with AI coding tools, releases Office clones too. Its users are asking for Autodesk replacements next. [155]

  4. 10 Oct 2026

    Community Linus Tech Tips has professional designers try PhotoCraft, ArtCraft’s open-source Photoshop clone built with AI, and asks whether clones like it are legal. [156]

  5. 3 Oct 2026

    Community redcolumn, a free, open-source PDF markup and takeoff app for construction drawings, runs in the browser or on Windows, Mac and Linux. It brings AutoCAD commands such as Offset and Fillet to markups, keeps files on the device, and saves markups as standard PDF annotations that open in Bluebeam. [157]

  6. 1 Oct 2026

    Community A Revit trainer releases BimGo, a first-person viewer for Revit models written, by its maker’s account, with Claude. [158]

  7. 29 Sep 2026

    Rayon raises a €10 million Series A. Its fourth version, due by the end of 2026, adds 3D, generative tools and AI agents to its 2D plans. [159]

  8. 15 Sep 2026

    Autodesk says Forma will replace Revit “over time”, with no date. Revit becomes a Forma Connected Client, and Autodesk says it will keep developing it in the meantime. [160]

  9. 8 Sep 2026

    Motif releases Motif Design, browser BIM in which AI agents work in the same model as the team. Revit and Rhino models stream in, and nothing exports back to Revit yet. [161]

  10. 12 Aug 2026

    Epic Games sells ArtStation, the artists’ portfolio site, and Sketchfab, the 3D model library, to KitBash. Epic keeps its Fab marketplace. [162]

  11. Jul 2026

    NeoBIM, the AI design company led by Enscape co-founder Moritz Luck, shows its buildOS tools at the AIA 2026 expo. [163]

  12. 17 Jun 2026

    Autodesk publishes a Revit MCP server, an official way for AI assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT to query Revit 2027 models. The official release is a read-only technical preview. [164]

  13. 2 Jun 2026

    Graphisoft announces a browser-based collaboration layer across the Nemetschek tools, with early access in 2026, and a native Archicad connection to Autodesk Forma. [165]

  14. 13 May 2026

    At NXT BLD in London, Arcol, Motif, Snaptrude, Qonic, Hypar and Autodesk share one BIM 2.0 panel, and Graebert demonstrates AutoDrawings, generated 2D drawings for Revit and IFC models. [166]

  15. 9 Apr 2026

    Qonic adds drawing generation: annotated plans and sections from BIM or IFC models that update with the model, exported to DWG. [167]

  16. 7 Apr 2026

    Autodesk releases Forma Building Design for schematic design, included with Revit and the AEC Collection. [168]

  17. 7 Apr 2026

    Revit 2027 adds Autodesk Assistant, which takes plain-language requests, and previews Revit as a client of the Forma cloud. [169]

  18. Oct 2025

    Community Pascal Editor, a building editor in the browser that AI agents can drive, goes open source. [170]

  19. 25 Jun 2025

    Snaptrude 3.0 splits early design into four modes that work alone or together. [171]

  20. 2 Jun 2025

    Arcol launches publicly after a closed beta, for shared massing and feasibility in the browser. [172]

  21. 30 Jan 2025

    Motif, founded by former Autodesk co-CEO Amar Hanspal, raises $46 million. Its first product, for reviewing models in the browser, follows in March. [173]

Also in development

Skema connects schematic design with native Revit models, with a Rhino plugin for geometry exchange. Swapp automates modeling and construction documentation inside Revit and Archicad. Nemetschek Creator is Graphisoft’s browser tool for early design, currently in early access.
